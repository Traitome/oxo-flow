# Human-Factors Evaluation Protocol

> **Status**: pre-registration draft only. No participant data, usability result,
> effect estimate, or comparative conclusion exists.

## 1. Purpose

The study asks whether supported workflow-authoring and debugging interactions
differ in time to a verified working pipeline, error comprehension, cognitive
load, and usability across pinned versions of Nextflow, Snakemake, and
`oxo-flow`. It is not designed to assume that any platform is superior.

The primary question is:

> Among eligible participants completing matched tasks in a counterbalanced
> within-subject design, what is the effect of workflow platform on the
> pre-registered primary outcome, with what uncertainty and missing-data
> sensitivity?

## 2. Evidence Status

This document defines a future study. It cannot establish usability from:

- synthetic fixtures;
- expert demonstration;
- developer dogfooding;
- benchmark task timing by the implementation team;
- screenshots, testimonials, or qualitative anecdotes.

The values produced by the synthetic SUS harness are test vectors for arithmetic
and reporting code, not pilot measurements or projections of study outcomes.

## 3. Study Population

Eligibility, exclusion, and stratification must be fixed before recruitment.
The target population is researchers or engineers who author or maintain
computational biological workflows.

Pre-register:

- minimum and maximum experience with each workflow system;
- education, role, and domain-experience strata;
- prior use of containers, schedulers, and workflow managers;
- accessibility accommodations;
- conflicts of interest, including prior contribution to any evaluated tool;
- withdrawal, incomplete-session, and protocol-deviation rules.

Do not assign a convenience sample of `N=30` without justification. Estimate
the sample size from the smallest effect of interest, within-person correlation,
expected missingness, and simulation of the pre-registered analysis model. The
sample-size analysis and its assumptions must be archived before recruitment.

## 4. Design

Use a randomized within-subject design with balanced platform order and a
pre-specified washout. Randomization is stratified by experience and biological
domain. Sequence allocation must be generated by an independent script or
service and concealed from outcome assessors.

All platforms receive:

- pinned software versions and container images;
- equivalent hardware, network, dataset, and resource limits;
- the same task instructions and permitted documentation;
- a fixed training and familiarization protocol;
- a declared time limit and rescue procedure;
- the same checkpoint and failure injection where the task requires it;
- a clean account or workspace to avoid cross-session state leakage.

Practice order, task order, and platform order must be recorded. Carryover must
be examined in the statistical analysis; a simple three-sequence Latin square
is insufficient without explicit washout and model assumptions.

## 5. Task Battery

Tasks are matched for scientific intent, required output, and available
documentation. Task equivalence must be reviewed independently before use.

### Task A: Authoring a Verified Pipeline

Build a paired-end RNA-seq workflow with read trimming, spliced alignment, and
transcript quantification. Success requires static validation and declared
outputs, not merely a syntactically valid file.

Record:

- time to first verified working pipeline (primary candidate outcome);
- task completion and abandonment;
- validation failures and manual edits;
- assistance requests;
- commands and tool versions used.

### Task B: Diagnosing a Biological Contract Mismatch

Provide an alignment artifact and rule that violate a declared sort-order, index,
read-group, or reference-build contract. Success requires identifying the
violation and producing a validated repair.

Record:

- time to correct diagnosis;
- correctness of the repair;
- unsafe or biologically invalid actions;
- trial executions and remediation latency.

### Task C: Recovering from an Infrastructure Failure

Inject a reproducible memory or scratch-pressure failure using the same
mechanism on every platform. Success requires preserving upstream completed work
where the platform supports checkpoints, applying an appropriate resource or
scratch change, and completing the task without altering biological semantics.

Record:

- recovery time and success;
- whether the displayed action matched the applied action;
- output and checkpoint integrity;
- manual edits and assistance.

## 6. Outcomes

The primary outcome must be singular, directional, and selected before data
collection. Pre-register one of task completion, time to verified completion, or
error-detection accuracy. The remaining measures are secondary or exploratory.

Secondary instruments and measures include:

- System Usability Scale (SUS), with the exact administered version and scoring
  code stored in the artifact;
- NASA Task Load Index, using a declared weighting procedure or raw subscales;
- number and type of validation or runtime errors;
- assistance and documentation use;
- recovery and data-integrity events;
- accessibility observations;
- qualitative interview themes reported without invented frequencies.

Record task-level raw data, not only aggregate means. Report failed, abandoned,
and rescued tasks.

## 7. Statistical Analysis Plan

Freeze the analysis plan before outcome inspection. At minimum, specify:

- the estimand and unit of analysis;
- the model for repeated measures, including platform, task, period, experience,
  and relevant interactions;
- transformation or non-parametric alternatives and their decision rules;
- cluster-robust or participant-level uncertainty;
- a pre-specified confidence level;
- a multiplicity strategy for secondary outcomes;
- handling of missing data, including sensitivity analyses;
- outlier, exclusion, and protocol-deviation rules;
- stopping and futility rules, if any.

Do not apply a paired t-test to SUS or other ordinal outcomes by default.
Parametric tests are acceptable only when their assumptions are stated and
checked; otherwise use a pre-specified robust or non-parametric analysis. A
non-significant result is reportable and is not evidence of equivalence unless
the design and analysis support an equivalence claim.

## 8. Blinding and Independence

Participant blinding is usually impossible when platform identity is visible.
Use independent outcome adjudication for recorded errors and repairs where
possible. The analyst should be blinded to platform labels until the primary
analysis is locked. At least one investigator independent of implementation must
review task equivalence, exclusions, code, and interpretation.

## 9. Ethics, Data, and Safety

Obtain ethics or institutional-review approval before recruitment. Archive:

- consent and withdrawal procedures;
- data-minimization and retention plan;
- de-identification and access controls;
- compensation disclosures;
- incident and distress procedures;
- protocol amendments and their approval;
- analysis code, environment lockfiles, and a controlled-access data dictionary.

No participant-identifiable data may be committed to the repository.

## 10. Reporting Requirements

The final report must include:

- recruitment flow and every exclusion;
- allocation and task-order balance checks;
- all task outcomes, including failures and abandoned work;
- raw-data provenance and analysis environment;
- effect sizes with confidence intervals;
- multiplicity-adjusted secondary findings;
- missing-data and outlier sensitivity analyses;
- negative, null, and contradictory findings;
- deviations from the pre-registered protocol;
- limitations on population, tasks, platforms, and ecological validity.

No usability, cognitive-load, migration-cost, or superiority claim may appear in
a roadmap, release note, slide, or manuscript until the registered analysis and
independent review are complete.
